**What this means for subsidiaries (legal analysis, not a court ruling):** § 2713 reaches any provider subject to US jurisdiction for data in its "possession, custody, or control". A US parent that controls an EU subsidiary is generally considered able to be compelled to produce that subsidiary's data. This is why SecNumCloud 3.2 requires EU ownership and control and forbids non-EU entities from holding control. It is also why Bleu and S3NS are structured as French-controlled joint ventures that license US technology. No published US court decision was found that directly compels an EU-incorporated subsidiary through its US parent under § 2713 (UNVERIFIED either way).

**Status on 2026-09-26:** the DPF is **still valid**. It was upheld at first instance, and an appeal is pending before the CJEU (C-703/25 P). Its independence pillars are weakened: the PCLOB has had no quorum since January 2025, and FTC commissioners have been removable at will since June 2026. The EDPB has formally asked the Commission to reassess. No suspension has been announced (UNVERIFIED past early September 2026). Businesses relying only on the DPF therefore carry a "Schrems III" risk: if the framework falls, transfers must stop or move to SCCs with a transfer impact assessment, with no grace period. There was none after Schrems II in 2020.

## Accurate plain-French explanation of the CLOUD Act (about 150 words)

Le CLOUD Act est une loi américaine de mars 2018. Il oblige tout fournisseur de services en ligne soumis au droit américain à remettre aux autorités judiciaires des États-Unis les données qu'il a « en sa possession, sous sa garde ou sous son contrôle ». Cette obligation vaut **quel que soit le pays où ces données sont stockées**. Un serveur situé à Paris ne protège donc pas des données confiées à Microsoft, Amazon ou Google. Une filiale européenne contrôlée par une maison mère américaine est aussi exposée. Le texte vise les enquêtes pénales et exige une procédure judiciaire américaine, par exemple un mandat délivré par un juge. Le fournisseur peut contester, mais la France n'a pas signé d'accord avec les États-Unis, et cette voie de recours reste donc très étroite. Le CLOUD Act n'est pas un outil de renseignement : la surveillance relève d'autres textes (FISA 702, EO 12333). En pratique, les demandes visant des entreprises européennes restent rares, mais elles existent, et aucun fournisseur américain ne peut garantir qu'il refusera.

## Common exaggerations to avoid

1. **"The CLOUD Act lets the NSA read all your data."** Wrong. The CLOUD Act concerns **criminal** law enforcement and needs US legal process (a warrant needs probable cause). Intelligence access goes through **FISA 702** and **EO 12333**. Keep the two separate.
2. **"The CLOUD Act created US access to foreign data."** It **clarified** § 2713 after the Microsoft Ireland case. The main new thing was the executive-agreement system. The extraterritorial "control" logic existed before (e.g. subpoena case law).
3. **"US authorities constantly grab European company data."** Microsoft's own figures (H2 2025) show **3** non-US enterprise customers with data outside the US had content disclosed, **1 in the EU/EFTA**. The risk is real but rare. Sell it as a **tail risk with severe impact** plus a **compliance and contract risk**, not as daily spying.
4. **"Microsoft admitted it hands over French data."** Carniaux said he **cannot guarantee** it won't happen and that it **had not happened yet**. Quote him exactly: « Non, je ne peux pas le garantir, mais, encore une fois, cela ne s'est encore jamais produit. »
5. **"Microsoft shut down the ICC."** Microsoft denies suspending ICC services. What is documented is that the **prosecutor lost his Microsoft mailbox** after sanctions and moved to Proton. Say "lost access to his e-mail after US sanctions (AP)". Do not say "Microsoft turned off the ICC".
6. **"Nayara proves the US kill switch."** Those were **EU** sanctions. Use it as an example of vendor compliance cut-offs in general, not as US coercion.
7. **"The DPF has been struck down" / "Schrems III has happened."** False as of 2026-09-26. The DPF is valid, upheld by the General Court (2025-09-03), with an appeal pending (C-703/25 P). Say "fragile", "under review", "contested".
8. **"FISA 702 expired, so the risk is gone."** The statute lapsed in June 2026, but the **certifications run until about March 2027**. Collection continues.
9. **"Hosting in the EU / EU Data Boundary / a 'sovereign' region solves it."** Location does not change jurisdiction under § 2713. At the same time, do not claim **every** US-technology offer is exposed: **S3NS PREMI3NS is SecNumCloud 3.2 qualified** (December 2025), and ANSSI treats that as immune to non-EU law.
10. **"SecNumCloud is required for all companies."** It is required for the **state, its operators and listed GIPs handling sensitive data** (SREN art. 31, décret 2026-272), and is recommended elsewhere. Private companies are not obliged, except indirectly through contracts or sector rules.
11. **"NIS2 fines are already in force in France."** The French law is **not yet adopted** (Assemblée séance publique on 2026-10-07). Say "jusqu'à 10 M€ ou 2 % du CA mondial prévus par la directive NIS2, en cours de transposition".
12. **"GDPR fines for US transfers hit SMEs."** The big fines hit Meta and Uber. For SMEs the realistic costs are **forced tool changes, audits, TIAs, lost tenders**. Do not imply that SMEs routinely get fined for using US SaaS.
13. **Numbers**: avoid "72% licence increase in Denmark" (UNVERIFIED). For SecNumCloud qualified-provider counts, cite the ANSSI catalogue on the day you publish.

## Top 10 marketing-usable facts (each with a source to cite)

1. **Under oath before the French Senate (10 June 2025), Microsoft France's legal director said: « Non, je ne peux pas le garantir »** when asked whether French data could be handed to US authorities without France's consent. (senat.fr compte rendu 2025-06-09 week)
2. **US law (18 U.S.C. § 2713) requires US providers to disclose data "regardless of whether [it] is located within or outside of the United States."** A Paris data centre does not change that. (law.cornell.edu)
3. **France has no CLOUD Act agreement with the US**, so the statutory objection route in the Act is not available for French customers' data. (18 U.S.C. § 2703(h))
4. **EU–US data transfer frameworks have been struck down twice in five years** (Safe Harbor 2015, Privacy Shield 2020). The third, the DPF, is under appeal at the CJEU, and in July 2026 the EDPB asked the Commission to reassess it. (curia.europa.eu; edpb.europa.eu)
