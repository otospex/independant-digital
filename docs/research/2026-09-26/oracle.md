## 2. Audits (LMS / GLAS) and landmark disputes

### How Oracle describes its audit function
- Oracle's page (fetched 2026-09-26) says LMS "serves as the sole Oracle licensing authority that can verify your licensing requirements… in a clear, objective, and independent manner". It calls LMS "an independent authority, separate from any ongoing commercial activities". It offers an "Assurance Service" (customer-led) and an "Audit Service" ("LMS-initiated and LMS-driven, comprehensive scope"). Source: https://www.oracle.com/corporate/license-management-services/
- Oracle's standard audit clause is reported to give 45 days' notice, require cooperation, and require payment of any excess-use fees within 30 days, with termination possible for non-payment. **UNVERIFIED** here (the OMA PDF could not be fetched). Check against the client's own OMA/OLSA before quoting.
- The **Partitioning Policy** (in effect 14 Feb 2022) classes **VMware and Oracle VM as "soft partitioning"**, which "is not permitted as a means to determine or limit the number of software licenses required". Oracle's own **Oracle Linux KVM and Oracle VM Server may count as hard partitioning** when cores are pinned. The document itself says it "is for educational purposes only… may not be incorporated into any contract and does not constitute a contract". Source: https://www.oracle.com/assets/partitioning-070609.pdf. House of Brick argues that sub-capacity on VMware is a contractual right: https://houseofbrick.com/blog/oracle-licensing-soft-partitioning-on-vmware-is-your-contractual-right-2/

### Audit-to-sales pattern: how to state it
- **Court-documented (France):** the Paris Court of Appeal (10 May 2016) found Oracle used its audit right twice "de manière à faire pression" to obtain new licence purchases. This is the strongest publishable statement, but it concerns **one case**.
- **Analyst-documented:** Gartner (2023) links Java audits to monetisation. Palisade reported formal audit letters to Fortune 100 firms. Jisc confirmed audit requests came before a collective purchase deal that waived historic fees. Tactical Law Group describes friendly outreach, then escalation, then formal audit.
- **Oracle's position:** LMS is "independent… separate from any ongoing commercial activities." Quote it for balance.

## Claims to avoid or soften (legal risk)

- **"Oracle holds customers hostage," "extortion," "racket," "scam".** Do not state these as fact. Quote customers, CIGREF ("inadmissible") or courts ("mauvaise foi et déloyauté", AFPA only), with attribution and a date.
- **"Oracle audits are sales tools" as a general rule.** Say "one French appellate court found Oracle used audits to pressure a customer (AFPA, 2016)" and "analysts report…". Also quote Oracle's statement that LMS is independent of sales.
- **"Oracle lost AFPA definitively."** Only the 2014 and 2016 rulings are verified; the cassation outcome is UNVERIFIED.
- **Mars "won" against Oracle.** It settled on confidential terms with no ruling. House of Brick's view that the result was favourable to Mars is opinion.
- **Rimini Street.** Do not present Rimini as a victim or Oracle as the loser. Oracle won the core 2015 copyright case, and Rimini was held in contempt. The 2024 appeal partly favoured Rimini, and the 2025 settlement has Rimini exiting PeopleSoft support.
- **"Java costs 5× more for everyone."** Gartner's 2–5× applies to large organisations it advised. Oracle's list also has tiers down to $5.25, and Gartner noted some best cases are cheaper. Say "up to".
- **Percentages from Redress, Licenseware, "oraclelicensingexperts" and similar advisory blogs** (35–45 audits, 7–12% uplifts, 60–90% repricing loss, 73% audited). These firms sell audit defence, so use them only as attributed opinion or not at all. The 73% figure is UNVERIFIED.
- **The "GLAS" rename.** It is reported by a law firm, but Oracle's site still says LMS. Write "reportedly renamed".
- **Cover Oregon.** This was a project-delivery dispute with countersuits, not licensing. The figures are from Wikipedia only. Leave it out, or clearly label it as context.
- **Regulators.** Do not imply that the EU, the CMA or French authorities have sanctioned Oracle's licensing. None was found. The GSA settlement was a settlement "without admission" (check the DOJ wording before saying "Oracle admitted").
- **OCI "lock-in".** Oracle also runs its database inside Azure, AWS and Google, so present the OCI incentives as factual price asymmetries from Oracle's own documents, not as coercion.
- **Birmingham City Council's Oracle ERP overrun** (UK) is often cited, but it is an implementation dispute and was not researched here. Avoid.
- **Oracle's documents** carry "for educational purposes only / may not be reproduced" notices. Quote short excerpts with a link, and do not reproduce tables wholesale.
- **Oracle's counterpoints, for fairness.** Oracle OpenJDK is free under GPL, Oracle JDK 21/25 is free under NFTC for a window, the Java price list is public, and LMS offers a customer-led "Assurance Service".
