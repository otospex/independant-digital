## 5. Egress & Data Act

### What the EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) guarantees. Text checked on EUR-Lex.
Source: [P] https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj

- **Applies from 12 September 2025** (Art. 50). The switching chapter (VI) applies to all "data processing services" (IaaS, PaaS, **SaaS**), **whatever the provider's nationality**. EU providers such as OVHcloud and Scaleway are bound too.
- **Art. 23: no obstacles to switching.** Providers "shall not impose and shall remove pre-commercial, commercial, technical, contractual and organisational obstacles". These cover terminating, signing with another provider, porting data, and unbundling.
- **Art. 25: mandatory contract terms.** The customer may give **at most two months' notice** to start switching. The provider must complete the transition **within 30 calendar days**, extendable to at most 7 months if technically justified. The provider must keep the service running and help with exit. The customer gets at least 30 days to retrieve data, and the provider must fully erase it afterwards.
- **Art. 29: switching charges phased out.**
  - From 11 Jan 2024 to 12 Jan 2027, only "reduced" switching charges are allowed, capped at costs "directly linked to the switching process".
  - **From 12 January 2027: no switching charges at all.** "Switching charges" explicitly include **data egress charges** (Art. 2(36)).
  - They exclude "standard service fees or early termination penalties". **The Data Act does not ban early-termination fees on fixed-term contracts.**
  - Providers must disclose fees and penalties before signing (Art. 29(4)).
- **Art. 34: in-parallel (multi-cloud) use.** Egress may still be charged, but only to pass on costs "without exceeding such costs".
- **Art. 31: exemptions.** Custom-built services not offered at broad scale are exempt from the charges ban. Non-production trial versions are exempt from the whole chapter.
- **What it does NOT do:** it does not regulate the level of subscription prices or prevent a provider from raising them.

## 7. Legal framing: safe claims vs claims to avoid

### What the law actually says

**French contract law (Code civil).** Texts quoted from knowledge; verify the exact wording on Légifrance (Code civil: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/texte_lc/LEGITEXT000006070721).

- **Art. 1103 / 1193.** Contracts bind the parties and "ne peuvent être modifiés ou révoqués que du consentement mutuel des parties, ou pour les causes que la loi autorise." So a price cannot change during a fixed term unless the contract allows it. This rule is not specific to the EU: US vendors also usually raise prices *at renewal* or under a clause the customer accepted (e.g. Google's "may change Fees at any time" clause).
- **Art. 1164 (contrats-cadre).** The price may be fixed unilaterally by one party, "à charge pour elle d'en motiver le montant en cas de contestation". If the price is set abusively, the judge can award damages and, where appropriate, terminate the contract. It is a remedy after the fact, not a ban.
- **Art. 1171 (contrats d'adhésion).** A non-negotiable clause creating a "déséquilibre significatif" is deemed unwritten. However, the assessment "ne porte ni sur l'objet principal du contrat ni sur l'adéquation du prix à la prestation". **The level of the price itself cannot be challenged under 1171.** A clause giving one side an unconditional right to change the price is arguably challengeable (**to be confirmed by counsel**).

**Code de commerce, L442-1.** Verify on Légifrance: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/texte_lc/LEGITEXT000005634379

- **I, 2°.** A party engages its liability if it subjects or tries to subject a commercial partner to obligations creating a "déséquilibre significatif". French courts have accepted that this can cover price terms. The Cour de cassation's *Galec* ruling of 25 Jan 2017 (no. 15-23.547) is usually cited: **UNVERIFIED citation, check**.
- **II.** "Rupture brutale" of an established commercial relationship without adequate written notice (liability capped at an 18-month notice). A sudden, drastic change to terms can sometimes count as a *partial* rupture.
- Caveats:
  - These are liability actions (damages) brought afterwards, often slowly. They do not block the increase.
  - In international contracts the foreign governing-law clause may defeat them. Following the CJEU's *Granarolo* ruling (C-196/15, 2016), rupture-brutale claims between parties with a tacit contractual relationship are treated as contractual, which strengthens a foreign choice of law.
  - Whether L442-1 is an overriding mandatory rule ("loi de police", Rome I Art. 9) is debated and has had mixed treatment in case law: **UNVERIFIED, consult a lawyer**.

**Consumer vs business.** French consumer law blacklists clauses reserving to the professional the right to change the price unilaterally (Code de la consommation R212-1: **UNVERIFIED exact item number**). It protects consumers and certain non-professionals, **not companies**. Most B2B customers cannot rely on it.

**Governing law and forum in US vendors' standard terms.**
- Google Workspace/Cloud: **California law, Santa Clara County courts**, with no EEA variant in the regional-modifications page. [P] https://workspace.google.com/terms/premier_terms/ ; https://cloud.google.com/terms/regional-modifications
- Microsoft commercial agreements for EEA customers are generally contracted with Microsoft Ireland Operations Ltd under Irish law: **UNVERIFIED** (the MCA text could not be fetched). Check the customer's own MCA, EA or MOSA.
- Enterprise customers can sometimes negotiate these terms. SMEs on click-through terms cannot.

**Data Act, Art. 13 (unfair terms, B2B).**
- It covers only terms "concerning access to and the use of data or liability and remedies" for data-related obligations.
- Art. 13(5)(g) presumes unfair a term letting the stronger party "substantially change the price specified in the contract" **in the context of data sharing** without a valid reason and a right to terminate.
- Art. 13(8) excludes "the adequacy of the price".
- It applies to contracts concluded after 12 Sep 2025. Pre-existing long-term contracts are covered from 12 Sep 2027 in some cases (Art. 50).
- **It is not a general ban on SaaS price increases.** Do not present it as one.

**Data Act switching rules (Arts. 23–29).** These are the real customer protection. The customer can leave on at most 2 months' notice, the provider must complete migration within 30 days, and there are no switching or egress charges from 12 Jan 2027. The practical effect: a price increase can be answered by leaving.

**DMA (Regulation (EU) 2022/1925).**
- No cloud service is designated yet. On 25 Jun 2026 the Commission *preliminarily* found AWS and Azure should be gatekeepers, citing "lock-in effects and high switching costs". Final designation is pending, followed by 6 months to comply. [R] https://www.theregister.com/legal/2026/06/25/european-commission-lines-up-amazon-and-microsoft-for-cloud-gatekeeper-status/5262127
- The DMA's obligations concern interoperability, data access, self-preferencing and tying. **It does not control price levels.**

**EU competition law (Art. 102 TFEU).** "Excessive/abusive prices" by a dominant firm can be illegal. That is exactly what the Commission is examining at Broadcom since Feb 2026. It applies to any dominant firm, EU or US, and only after a long procedure.

### Safe formulations (usable on the site, in French)

1. « En Europe, un prix ne peut pas changer en cours de contrat sans votre accord ou sans une clause qui le prévoit (art. 1193 du Code civil). Chez un prestataire français, ce contrat relève du droit français et de tribunaux français. »
2. « Les conditions standard de plusieurs éditeurs américains désignent un droit et un tribunal étrangers — par exemple le droit californien et les tribunaux du comté de Santa Clara pour Google Workspace — ce qui rend toute contestation plus longue et plus coûteuse pour une entreprise française. »
3. « Depuis le 12 septembre 2025, le Data Act vous garantit de pouvoir quitter un fournisseur cloud avec un préavis de deux mois au plus, et à partir du 12 janvier 2027, sans frais de sortie. Face à une hausse, vous avez désormais une vraie porte de sortie. »
4. « Les fournisseurs européens augmentent aussi leurs prix — OVHcloud l'a fait en 2022 et 2026, Hetzner en 2026, en invoquant l'énergie puis le prix de la mémoire. La différence tient au cadre : droit applicable, juridiction, transparence et réversibilité. »
5. « Quand un fournisseur en position dominante impose des hausses massives, le droit européen de la concurrence permet d'agir : c'est ce qu'examine la Commission européenne pour Broadcom/VMware depuis 2026. »

### Claims to AVOID

- ❌ « Les entreprises européennes ne peuvent pas augmenter leurs prix comme elles veulent / sur un coup de tête. » This is false: EU vendors raise prices at renewal, and some raised them mid-relationship with notice (OVHcloud 2022, Hetzner 2026).
- ❌ « La loi européenne interdit les hausses de prix unilatérales » / « Le Data Act interdit les hausses de prix. » The Data Act regulates switching and data-related terms, and Art. 13(8) excludes price adequacy.
- ❌ « Le DMA vous protège contre les hausses. » No cloud gatekeeper is designated yet, and the DMA does not regulate price levels.
- ❌ « Les hausses de Microsoft/Google sont illégales. » They are announced in advance, apply at renewal and follow the contracts. Only Broadcom is under formal EU antitrust examination for "abusive prices", and that is an allegation. ACCC v Microsoft is a pending consumer case in Australia.
- ❌ « Le droit français s'applique toujours, même avec un fournisseur américain. » It depends on the governing-law clause and on arguments about overriding mandatory rules.
- ❌ Stating "+1,500% chez VMware" as a general fact. Always attribute it to CISPE members or AT&T, and call it a claim.
- ❌ "Free exit from AWS/Azure/Google" without the conditions: credits on request, full exit, a time limit (60 days for Azure, extended to 180 in the UK context).
